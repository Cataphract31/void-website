Raffle model. Every tick inside earns tickets, faster while the reactor is hot, and they carry between rounds. One holder takes the whole pool and all tickets void — the next raffle starts from zero for everyone. Median gap between hits is 1,069 rounds; the longest observed drought ran 10,166.

Revenue is 4% of handle every round with no variance and no bankroll — payouts are carved from stakes already in the contract, so no round, streak, or whale can produce a loss. Revenue scales with concurrency and round frequency rather than bet size.

Why it holds up

  • Provably solvent. Every payout is money already destroyed that tick. Nothing to back, nothing to drain.
  • No dominant strategy. Expected value per tick is exactly 1 regardless of danger level — a mathematical property, not a tuning result.
  • Manipulation-proof. Cooling the reactor doesn't improve your odds, so multi-wallet attacks earn nothing.
  • Fixed entry. Nobody can be a larger share of the pot than anyone else, which removes the size penalty inherent to pot-based games.
  • Batched exits. Everyone in a tick clears together, so latency and bots hold no edge over humans.
  • Live spectacle. Every tick, real players visibly die and their SOL flies into the survivors — the mechanic explains itself with no tutorial.

Deployment notes

  • Session-key vault: one signature per session, non-custodial, no wallet popup per action.
  • One settlement transaction per round; exits are signed intents batched at tick boundaries.
  • Randomness from a hash tape committed before each round — verifiable after, unknowable during.
  • Jackpot state is one balance and one cycle number per wallet; winner is deterministic from public data.
  • Larger lobbies raise the ceiling — max win scales linearly with headcount (28 → 26×, 80 → 74×, 150 → 139×). Size lobbies for round pacing (32s at 28 players, 43s at 150), not for payout reasons.
  • Jackpot requires pre-funding at launch; it needs volume to become worth chasing.

All figures from Monte Carlo simulation of the shipped parameters. Base-game ceiling scales linearly with lobby size (28 players → 26×; 80 → 74×; 150 → 139×) because the last player standing takes the whole room. Jackpot is uncapped.
